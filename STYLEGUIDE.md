# STYLEGUIDE.md
## Translation of *Revelations of Divine Love* — Julian of Norwich
### A Working Guide for AI-Assisted Translation

---

## 1. Project Identity

This is a new English translation of Julian of Norwich's *Revelations of Divine Love*
(also known as *Showings*), prepared for a illustrated devotional edition. The
translation aims to be **faithful, literary, and living** — neither archaically
costumed nor flatly modernised. The intended reader is spiritually serious and
literate, but not necessarily a medieval scholar.

The translation works primarily from the **Long Text** (*A Revelation of Love*),
using **Sloane 2499** (British Library) as base text, with reference to the Paris
manuscript (BnF fonds anglais 40) where readings diverge. Sloane is preferred for
its greater closeness to Julian's fourteenth-century vocabulary and syntax, and
because the soundest freely available scholarly editions of the Middle English are
built upon it. Substantive textual choices must be logged in `NOTES.md`.

---

## 2. Source Text

- **Base text:** Long Text of the *Revelations*, c. 1393, from **Sloane 2499**
  (British Library)
- **Working Middle English source:** TEAMS Middle English Texts Series (METS),
  ed. Marion Glasscoe lineage — freely available, editorially sound, with glossary.
  Filed chapter by chapter in `SOURCE.md`.
- **Variant reference:** Paris manuscript (BnF fonds anglais 40) where readings
  meaningfully diverge.
- **Modern reference editions (for checking, NOT translating from):** Edmund
  Colledge & James Walsh (Paulist Press, 1978); Nicholas Watson & Jacqueline
  Jenkins (Penn State, 2006).
- **Public-domain modernisation (reference gloss only):** Grace Warrack (1901),
  filed in `REFERENCE.md`. See warning below.

### 2.1 Do Not Translate From Warrack
The widely circulated free texts (Project Gutenberg, CCEL) are predominantly Grace
Warrack's 1901 *modernised* edition, not the raw Middle English. Warrack lightly
modernised spelling and smoothed syntax. Her text is useful as a sanity check —
where our reading diverges sharply from hers, pause and reconsider — but it is a
predecessor's translation, not the source. **Always translate from the Middle
English in `SOURCE.md`, never from `REFERENCE.md`.**

---

## 3. The Governing Tone

Julian writes from within a state of contemplative recollection. Her prose is:

- **Warm but not sentimental.** The love she describes is overwhelming and cosmic,
  not cosy.
- **Precise but not clinical.** She is a careful theologian making exact
  distinctions, but always in the register of personal witness.
- **Accumulative.** She builds meaning by circling back, adding, qualifying. Do not
  iron this out into linearity.
- **Wondering.** There is perpetual astonishment in her voice. Preserve it.

The translation should feel as though it could be read aloud in a quiet chapel or
used in *lectio divina*. It should not feel like a critical edition or a self-help
book.

---

## 4. Rhythm and Syntax

### 4.1 Sentence Length
Julian's sentences are often long and paratactic, joined by *and*, *for*, *but*,
*which*. **Do not systematically break these into short modern sentences.** The
accumulative rhythm is theologically expressive — it enacts the way insight deepens
by layering.

However, where a sentence is genuinely confusing in its original structure, light
syntactic clarification is permitted, and must be logged in `NOTES.md`.

**Source:**
> *For I am sure that there is no man that needeth to seek further than He, till he
> hath Him that He seeketh: and He is God.*

**Preferred rendering:**
> For I am certain that no one need seek further than him, until they have found
> the one they seek — and that one is God.

**Avoid:**
> No one needs to look further than God. He is all we seek.

### 4.2 Pauses and Breath
Use the em dash sparingly, and only for genuine syntactic suspension — not as a
general-purpose connector. Julian's pauses are better served by the comma and the
colon.

### 4.3 Relative Clauses
Prefer *who* over *that* for persons, including divine persons. "God, who is our
ground" not "God, that is our ground."

---

## 5. Vocabulary Register

### 5.1 General Principle
The vocabulary should draw from the **deep well of English** — Anglo-Saxon roots,
liturgical memory, words that have been worn smooth by centuries of use. Avoid:

- Therapy-register words (*healing journey*, *processing*, *space*)
- Management-register words (*framework*, *resource*, *capacity*)
- Academic hedging (*arguably*, *we might say*, *in some sense*)
- Arch pseudo-medievalisms (*dost*, *thee* used inconsistently, *lo*)

### 5.2 Permitted Archaisms
Some older forms may be retained where they carry irreplaceable weight, and where
the meaning is transparent:

- *Behold* (preferred over *look at* or *observe*)
- *Sorrow* (preferred over *grief* in most contexts)
- *Dwell* (preferred over *remain* or *stay*)
- *Longing* (preferred over *desire* in affective contexts)
- *Gracious* (retain Julian's own use of this; do not modernise to *kind*)

## 6. Key Term Glossary

These are Julian's load-bearing theological terms. Each has a ruling translation
that must be applied consistently. Exceptions require a `NOTES.md` entry.

| Middle English | Ruling Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| *soule* | soul | Not "self" or "psyche" |
| *goostly* | spiritual | Not "ghostly" (misleading) |
| *kinde* / *kindly* | nature / natural | Also carries "kind/kindness" — context determines; log ambiguous cases |
| *homely* / *homeliness* | intimacy / intimate | NOT "homely" (modern meaning is derogatory); consider "familiar" where "intimate" feels too romantic |
| *oneing* | oneing | Retain Julian's own coinage; do not translate as "union" — she chose a verbal noun deliberately |
| *privy* / *privily* | secret / secretly | Or "inward/inwardly" where *privy* has an interior rather than hidden sense |
| *marvelling* | wondering | Not "marvelling" (too archaic) nor "amazed" (too sudden); "wondering" preserves the sustained quality |
| *courteous* / *courtesy* | courteous / courtesy | Retain; Julian's use is technical and theological (divine condescension-as-nobility) |
| *rest* | rest | Retain; do not translate as "peace" — Julian distinguishes them |
| *bliss* | bliss | Retain; do not flatten to "joy" or "happiness" |
| *failing* | falling short / diminishing | Context-dependent; log each instance |
| *sensuality* | sensory nature | Julian means the bodily-experiential dimension of the soul, not sexuality |
| *substance* | substance | Retain the scholastic term; do not paraphrase |
| *shewing* / *shewings* | showing / showings | Use "showing(s)" — this is the established English rendering and carries the sense of revelation-as-gift |
| *motherhood* (of God/Christ) | motherhood | Do not soften or bracket; this is central to Julian's theology |

## 7. Handling the Divine Persons

### 7.1 Pronouns
Use **he/him** for Christ and God the Father, following Julian's own usage and the
devotional tradition of this text. Do not alter to gender-neutral pronouns — this
is a translation, not an adaptation. If a future adapted edition is produced, that
is a separate project.

**He** and **Him** may be capitalised when referring unambiguously to a divine
person, at the translator's discretion, to preserve devotional register.

### 7.2 Christ's Humanity
Julian moves fluidly between Christ's divinity and his bodily suffering. Do not
smooth this over. The shift from transcendence to wound is the point.

### 7.3 The Motherhood Passages (Chapters 57–63)
These require particular care. Julian's language is not metaphorical decoration —
it is theological argument. Translate with full weight. Do not add scare quotes,
hedging clauses, or editorial softening in the translation itself (commentary
belongs in `NOTES.md`).

---

## 8. What Claude Code Must Not Do

- Do not paraphrase Julian's meaning into clearer propositional statements. Render
  her syntax, then clarify in notes if needed.
- Do not resolve Julian's deliberate paradoxes. "All shall be well" alongside a
  frank account of sin is not a contradiction to be harmonised.
- Do not add connective tissue Julian did not supply. If she begins a new thought
  abruptly, begin it abruptly.
- Do not substitute a single modern word where Julian uses a compound or a
  repetition for emphasis.
- Do not translate *alle shalle be wele* as anything other than some form of **"all
  shall be well"** — this phrase has entered the language and any deviation requires
  explicit justification in `NOTES.md`.

## 10. Iterative Refinement Protocol

1. Claude Code produces a draft translation of the given chapter.
2. The translator (Mark) reviews against source and styleguide.
3. Amendments are made either to the translation or to this STYLEGUIDE.
4. If a STYLEGUIDE amendment is made, it propagates retroactively — earlier chapters
   must be reviewed for consistency.
5. The GLOSSARY.md is updated whenever a new term ruling is established.

---

## 11. A Note on the Translator's Voice

This edition is made by someone formed in the Hesychast and Carmelite contemplative
traditions, with deep familiarity with the apophatic and kataphatic streams Julian
inhabits simultaneously. The translation should reflect that formation — not
intrusively, but as the natural key in which it is pitched. Julian is a theologian
of *theosis* writing before that word was available to her in English. The
translation should be alive to that.
