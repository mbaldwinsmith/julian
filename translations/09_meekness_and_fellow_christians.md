# Chapter 9 — Meekness and the Love of Fellow Christians

## Source (Sloane 2499, Middle English)

**Scribal summary (historical layer):** *Of the mekenes of this woman kepeing hir alway in the feith of Holy Church; and how he that lovyth his evyn Cristen for God lovith all thing. Ninth chapter.*

> Middle English running text held in `SOURCE.md` (Chapter 9); translated from there.

## Translation

For [in] the showing I am not good but if I love God the better; and in as much as you love God the better, it is more to you than to me. I say this not to them that be wise, for they know it well; but I say it to you that be simple, for ease and comfort — for we are all one in comfort. For truly it was not shown me that God loved me better than the least soul that is in grace; for I am sure that there are many that never had showing nor sight, but [had] of the common teaching of Holy Church, that love God better than I. For if I look singularly to myself, I am right nothing; but in general I am, in hope, in oneness of charity with all my fellow Christians. For in this oneness stands the life of all mankind that shall be saved.

For God is all that is good, as to my sight; and God has made all that is made, and God loves all that he has made. And he that generally loves all his fellow Christians for God, he loves all that is. For in mankind that shall be saved is comprehended all — that is to say, all that is made, and the Maker of all; for in man is God, and God is in all. And I hope, by the grace of God, that he who beholds it thus shall be truly taught and mightily comforted, if he needs comfort.

I speak of them that shall be saved, for in this time God showed me none other. But in all thing I believe as Holy Church believes, preaches, and teaches. For the faith of Holy Church — which I had beforehand understood, and, as I hope, by the grace of God wilfully kept in use and custom — stood continually in my sight, my willing and meaning never to receive anything that might be contrary thereunto. And with this intent I beheld the showing with all my diligence; for in all this blessed showing I beheld it as one in God's meaning. All this was shown by three [means]: that is to say, by bodily sight, and by word formed in my understanding, and by spiritual sight. But the spiritual sight — I cannot, nor may not, show it as openly nor as fully as I would. But I trust in our Lord God Almighty that he shall, of his goodness, and for your love, make you take it more spiritually and more sweetly than I can or may tell it.

## Translator's Notes

- **§6/rulings applied:** *shewing* → showing; *gostly* → spiritual; *evyn Cristen* →
  "fellow Christians"; divine/reader address modern *you*.
- ***onehede* → "oneness"** (one-hood/unity) — distinct from *oneing* (the verbal-noun
  coinage); rendered "oneness of charity."
- ***leve / levith* → "believe / believes"** here (*"I believe as Holy Church believes"*).
- ***by thre* → "by three [means]"** — the three modes of showing (bodily sight, word,
  spiritual sight), named just below.
