# Chapter 2 — Three Petitions

## Source (Sloane 2499, Middle English)

**Scribal summary (historical layer):** *The second chapter. Of the tyme of these revelations, and how shee asked three petitions.*

These Revelations were shewed to a simple creature that cowde no letter the yeere of our Lord 1373, the eighth day of May, which creature desired afore three gifts of God. The first was mende of His passion. The second was bodily sekenesse in youth at thirty yeeres of age. The third was to have of Gods gift three wounds. As in the first methought I had sume feleing in the passion of Christe, but yet I desired more be the grace of God. Methought I would have beene that time with Mary Magdalen and with other that were Crists lovers, and therefore I desired a bodily sight wherein I might have more knowledge of the bodily peynes of our Saviour, and of the compassion our Lady and of all His trew lovers that seene that time His peynes, for I would be one of them and suffer with Him. Other sight ner sheweing of God desired I never none till the soule was departid fro the body. The cause of this petition was that after the sheweing I should have the more trew minde in the passion of Christe.

The second came to my mynde with contrition frely desireing that sekenesse so herde as to deth that I might in that sekeness underfongyn alle my rites of Holy Church, myselfe weneing that I should dye, and that all creatures might suppose the same that seyen me, for I would have no manner comfort of eardtly life. In this sekenesse I desired to have all manier peynes bodily and ghostly that I should have if I should dye, with all the dreds and tempests of the fends, except the outpassing of the soule. And this I ment for I would be purged be the mercy of God and after lyven more to the worshippe of God because of that sekenesse; and that for the more speede in my deth, for I desired to be soone with my God.

These two desires of the passion and the sekenesse I desired with a condition, seying thus: "Lord, thou wotith what I would, if it be Thy will that I have it, and if it be not Thy will, good Lord, be not displeased, for I will nought, but as Thou wilt." For the third, by the grace of God and teachyng of Holy Church, I conceived a mighty desire to receive three wounds in my life; that is to sey, the wound of very contrition, the wound of kinde compassion, and the wound of willfull longing to God. And all this last petition I asked without any condition. These two desires foresaid passid fro my minde, and the third dwelled with me continually.

## Translation

These showings were shown to a simple creature who could not read a letter, in the year of our Lord 1373, the eighth day of May — which creature had desired, beforehand, three gifts of God. The first was the remembrance of his passion; the second was a bodily sickness in my youth, at thirty years of age; the third was to have, of God's gift, three wounds. As for the first, it seemed to me that I had some feeling of the passion of Christ, but still I desired more, by the grace of God. It seemed to me that I would have been, at that time, with Mary Magdalene and with the others who were Christ's lovers, and so I desired a bodily sight, in which I might have more knowledge of the bodily pains of our Saviour, and of the compassion of our Lady and of all his true lovers who saw, in that time, his pains; for I would be one of them and suffer with him. No other sight or showing of God did I ever desire, until the soul was departed from the body. The cause of this petition was that, after the showing, I should have the more true remembrance of the passion of Christ.

The second came to my mind with contrition — freely desiring that the sickness might be so hard as death, that I might, in that sickness, receive all my rites of Holy Church, myself supposing that I should die, and that all who saw me might suppose the same; for I would have no manner of comfort of earthly life. In this sickness I desired to have all manner of pains, bodily and spiritual, that I should have if I were to die — with all the dreads and tempests of the fiends — except the passing-out of the soul. And this I meant, for I would be purged by the mercy of God, and afterward live more to the worship of God because of that sickness; and this too for the more speed toward my death, for I desired to be soon with my God.

These two desires — of the passion and of the sickness — I desired with a condition, saying thus: "Lord, you know what I would; if it be your will that I have it, and if it be not your will, good Lord, be not displeased; for I will nothing but as you will." For the third, by the grace of God and the teaching of Holy Church, I conceived a mighty desire to receive three wounds in my life: that is to say, the wound of true contrition, the wound of kind compassion, and the wound of wilful longing for God. And all this last petition I asked without any condition. These two desires aforesaid passed from my mind, and the third dwelled with me continually.

## Translator's Notes

Julian's first running prose. The accumulative, paratactic rhythm (§4.1) is kept — long
sentences joined by *and*/*for*, not broken into modern short ones.

- **Glossary/§5.2 applied:** *sheweing(s)* → showing(s); *ghostly* → **spiritual** (not
  "ghostly"); *longing* and *dwelled* retained; *compassion*, *contrition*, *worship*
  kept in Julian's register.
- ***mende of His passion* → "remembrance of his passion":** *mende* = mind /
  recollection; "remembrance" over a bare "mind of his passion."
- ***cowde no letter* → "could not read a letter":** echoes the manuscript incipit held
  for the front matter. (Crampton notes it may also mean "knew no Latin"; the reading-sense
  is standard.)
- **Double negative kept lightly:** *"desired I never none"* → "No other sight or showing
  of God did I ever desire" — the emphatic fronting preserved, per §8 (don't flatten).

**Rulings applied (2026-06-20):**
- **Divine address → modern "you/your".** Julian's prayer reads *"Lord, you know what I
  would… as you will,"* not *thou/thee* (per §5.1; whole-book ruling).
- ***kinde compassion* → "kind compassion".** This instance leans on the *kind/kindness*
  sense of *kinde* rather than the §6 default *nature/natural*; logged in NOTES.

**Still flagged (minor):** ***willfull longing* → "wilful longing for God"** — Crampton
notes *will/wilful* are weighted in Julian (a sustained, fully conscious choosing, not
modern "stubborn"). If "wilful" reads wrong to you, alternatives: "whole-willed longing"
/ "longing for God with all my will."
