# Chapter 7 — Courtesy and Intimacy

## Source (Sloane 2499, Middle English)

**Scribal summary (historical layer):** *How our Lady, beholdyng the gretenes of hir Maker, thowte hirselfe leste; and of the great droppys of blode renning from under the garland; and how the most joy to man is that God most hie and mightie is holyest and curtesiest. Seventh chapter.*

> Middle English running text held in `SOURCE.md` (Chapter 7); translated from there,
> not duplicated here.

## Translation

And to teach us this — as to my understanding — our Lord God showed our Lady Saint Mary at the same time; that is to mean, the high wisdom and truth she had in beholding of her Maker: so great, so high, so mighty, and so good. This greatness and this nobility of the beholding of God filled her with reverent dread; and with this she saw herself so little and so low, so simple and so poor, in comparison with her Lord God, that this reverent dread filled her with meekness. And thus, by this ground, she was filled with grace and with all manner of virtues, and surpasses all creatures. In all the time that he showed this that I have said now, in spiritual sight I saw the bodily sight lasting, of the plenteous bleeding of the head. The great drops of blood fell down from under the garland like pellets, seeming as it had come out of the veins; and in the coming out they were brown-red, for the blood was full thick; and in the spreading abroad they were bright red; and when it came to the brows, then it vanished — notwithstanding, the bleeding continued till many things were seen and understood. The fairness and the liveliness is like nothing but the same. The plenteousness is like to the drops of water that fall off the eaves after a great shower of rain, that fall so thick that no one may number them with bodily wit; and for the roundness, they were like to the scale of a herring in the spreading on the forehead. These three came to my mind at the time: pellets, for the roundness in the coming out of the blood; the scale of herring, in the spreading on the forehead, for roundness; the drops of eaves, for the plenteousness innumerable. This showing was quick and living, and dreadful and fearsome, sweet and lovely.

And of all the sight, it was most comfort to me that our God and Lord, who is so reverend and to be held in awe, is so intimate and courteous; and this most filled me with delight and sureness of soul. And to the understanding of this, he showed this open example. It is the most worship that a solemn King or a great Lord may do to a poor servant, if he will be intimate with him — and especially if he shows it himself, of a full true meaning and with a glad cheer, both in private and in public. Then thinks this poor creature thus: "Ah, what might this noble Lord do, of more worship and joy to me, than to show me, that am so simple, this marvellous intimacy?" Truly, it is more joy and delight to me than if he gave me great gifts and were himself distant in manner. This bodily example was shown so high that the heart might be ravished, and almost forget itself for joy of this great intimacy. Thus it fares with our Lord Jesus and with us; for truly it is the most joy that may be, as to my sight, that he who is highest and mightiest, noblest and worthiest, is lowest and meekest, most intimate and most courteous. And truly and surely this marvellous joy shall be shown to us all when we see him. And this wills our Lord: that we will and trust, joy and delight, comfort and solace ourselves, as we may, with his grace and his help, until the time that we see it truly. For the most fullness of joy that we shall have, as to my sight, is the marvellous courtesy and intimacy of our Father, who is our Maker, in our Lord Jesus Christ, who is our brother and our Saviour.

But this marvellous intimacy may no one know in this time of life, unless he have it of special showing of our Lord, or of great plenty of grace inwardly given of the Holy Ghost. But faith and belief, with charity, deserve the reward; and so it is had by grace; for in faith, with hope and charity, our life is grounded. The showing, made to whom God will, plainly teaches the same, opened and declared with many secret points belonging to our faith which are worshipful to know. And when the showing, which is given in a time, is passed and hidden, then the faith keeps it by grace of the Holy Ghost unto our life's end. And thus it stands with the showing: it is no other than the faith, neither less nor more, as may be seen by our Lord's meaning, in the same matter, by the time it comes to the end.

## Translator's Notes

- **§6 applied — courtesy/courteous retained:** *homley and curtes* → "intimate and
  courteous"; *homlyest and curteysest* → "most intimate and most courteous"; *curtesie
  and homlyhede* → "courtesy and intimacy"; *mervelous homlyhede* → "marvellous intimacy".
- ***drede* family by context:** of the showing, *hidouse and dredfull* → "dreadful and
  fearsome"; of God, *so reverent and dredefull* → "so reverend and to be held in awe"
  (avoiding modern misread of "dreadful"); *reverend/reverent drede* → "reverent dread".
- ***mede* → "reward"**; *prive and partie* → "in private and in public"; *strange in
  maner* → "distant in manner".
- **§4.1 light clarification (logged):** the final sentence — *"And thus be the shewyng:
  It is not other than the faith…"* — was lightly clarified to "And thus it stands with
  the showing…" for readability; sense preserved.
