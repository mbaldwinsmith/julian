# A Note on the Text

This translation is made from the **Long Text** of Julian's *Revelation of Divine
Love*, composed around 1393. The Long Text survives principally in two witnesses: the
**Sloane manuscript** (Sloane 2499, British Library) and the **Paris manuscript**
(BnF fonds anglais 40).

**Base text.** This edition takes **Sloane 2499** as its base. Sloane stays closer to
Julian's fourteenth-century vocabulary and syntax, and the soundest freely available
scholarly editions of the Middle English rest upon it. Where the Paris manuscript
preserves a meaningfully different reading, that divergence has been weighed and, where
adopted, recorded.

**The scribal headings.** Sloane carries short summaries at the head of each chapter.
These are almost certainly the work of an early scribe rather than Julian herself. In
this edition they are kept but **set apart as a historical layer** — translated, yet
typographically distinguished from Julian's own words, so the reader can hear the
difference between the anchoress's voice and a later hand introducing her.

**On the translation.** Every word here is rendered from the Middle English, never from
a modern predecessor. Where Julian's syntax has been lightly clarified, or a load-bearing
term decided in a particular direction, the choice has been logged rather than made
silently.
